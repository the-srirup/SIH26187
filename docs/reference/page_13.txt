Part 8 — What judges will ask, and your answers
"Why not just buy cameras with built-in analytics?" Cost per post, multiplied by the number of posts
along a 15,000 km border. Also vendor lock-in — proprietary systems cannot be integrated with existing
command-and-control software. Have a specific rupee figure ready.
"How does this work without internet at a remote BOP?" Everything runs on a local edge device — a
Jetson Orin Nano or a small GPU mini-PC. Only alert metadata and short clips travel upstream, not
continuous video. Demonstrate this by running your whole system on an isolated hotspot with airplane
mode on your laptop.
"How many cameras can one device handle?" Give a measured number, not a guess. Benchmark it: run 1,
2, 4 streams and record the FPS. A real number from your own testing is far more convincing than a claim.
"What about false alarms?" Confidence thresholds, minimum object size, debounce windows, and
configurable per-camera rules. Show that you thought about the operator's experience, not just the
model's accuracy.
"Why is this filed under Blockchain & Cybersecurity?" This is your opening. Explain the tamper-evident
hash chain, the evidentiary value in a court or court-martial, and add RBAC, TLS, and audit logging.
"Is face recognition accurate enough?" Say honestly that at CCTV distances and angles it is not reliable
enough for positive identification, and that you present it as an investigative lead requiring human
confirmation. Overclaiming here is the fastest way to lose credibility with a Home Ministry panel.
